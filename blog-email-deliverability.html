Email deliverability: why your campaigns are landing in spam

Open rates dropping isn't always about your subject lines — sometimes it's technical, and fixable.

LF
Leo Fischer
Social & Content Lead · 8 min read

When open rates drop, most people's first instinct is to blame the subject line. Sometimes that's right. But often, the real problem is technical — your emails are landing in spam or a promotions tab before anyone even gets the chance to not open them.

The authentication basics most stores skip

SPF, DKIM, and DMARC are technical records that tell inbox providers your emails are genuinely coming from you, not being spoofed. Missing or misconfigured records are one of the most common, most fixable causes of poor deliverability we find — and most store owners have never even heard of them, let alone checked whether they're set up correctly.

Sender reputation is a real, trackable thing

Inbox providers track how recipients treat your emails over time — opens, clicks, and critically, spam complaints and deletions without opening. A poor reputation, once established, actively suppresses your future emails into spam folders even for genuinely interested subscribers, which is why deliverability problems tend to compound rather than stay flat.

List hygiene matters more than list size

Continuing to email addresses that never open, never click, and haven't engaged in months actively drags down your sender reputation with every send. A smaller, genuinely engaged list consistently outperforms a larger, stale one — both in open rates and, more importantly, in actual revenue per email sent.

Content patterns that trigger spam filters

ALL CAPS subject lines, excessive exclamation points, and image-only emails with little to no real text all read as spam-like patterns to modern filters — not because any single one is disqualifying, but because they cumulatively signal lower-quality sending behavior to increasingly sophisticated spam detection.

How we actually diagnose this for clients

We check authentication records first — a five-minute technical check that catches a surprising number of cases. Then we look at engagement trends over the last few months, not just the most recent campaign, since a slow decline usually points to reputation or list quality, while a sudden single-campaign drop usually points to that specific email's content or a technical send issue.

Key takeaways

  • Check SPF, DKIM, and DMARC records first — misconfiguration is a common, fixable cause of poor deliverability.
  • Sender reputation compounds — a smaller, engaged list consistently outperforms a larger, stale one.
  • ALL CAPS, excessive exclamation points, and image-only emails read as spam-like patterns to modern filters.
