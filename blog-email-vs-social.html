Email vs. social: where should a small store spend first?

Both matter eventually — but if you can only invest in one right now, here's how we'd decide.

By Leo Fischer · 7 min read

We get this question constantly from smaller stores working with a limited budget: email or social, first? The honest answer depends on where you already are — here's the framework we actually use with clients.

If you have an existing customer list, start with email

Email consistently delivers the highest return of any channel for stores with existing customers, because you already have permission and context — you know they've bought before. A well-built flow setup (welcome, abandoned cart, post-purchase) can start generating revenue within the first month, with no ongoing content production required once it's live.

If you're pre-revenue or very early, start with social

Without an existing list, email has nothing to send to yet. Social is where new-to-brand discovery happens — it's how you build the audience email will eventually convert. Early on, the job of social isn't sales, it's building enough of a following that your email list has somewhere to grow from.

The mistake we see most often

Stores split a small budget evenly across both and do neither well — a half-built flow setup and an inconsistent posting schedule. Two mediocre channels almost always underperform one channel done properly. Pick the one that matches your actual stage, do it well for a real stretch of time, then expand.

Our honest rule of thumb

Under 500 email subscribers with real purchase history: focus on social to grow that list. Over 500 engaged subscribers: shift real budget into email flows before adding more social spend. It's not a permanent choice — it's a sequencing decision, and getting the order right matters more than most people expect.
