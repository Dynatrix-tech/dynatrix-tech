The technical SEO audit we run on every new client

The exact checklist we use in week one, from crawl budget to canonical tags.

PN
Priya Nandakumar
SEO Strategist · 10 min read

Before we touch content or keywords, we spend the first week on the technical foundation. Content strategy built on top of a broken technical base almost never performs — so here's exactly what we check first.

Crawlability

Can Google actually reach and read your pages? We check robots.txt for accidental blocks, review the XML sitemap for accuracy, and crawl the site ourselves to catch orphaned pages — ones with no internal links pointing to them, which search engines struggle to find at all.

Indexing signals

We look for duplicate or conflicting canonical tags, accidental noindex tags left over from staging, and pages competing against each other for the same keyword — a common issue on stores with heavily filtered category pages.

This audit alone usually surfaces more revenue opportunity than a month of new content would.

Site speed and Core Web Vitals

Not just the homepage — we sample product pages, category pages, and the checkout flow, since Google evaluates real user experience across the whole site, not a single page.

Structured data

Missing or broken schema markup means you're leaving rich results — star ratings, pricing, FAQ dropdowns — on the table. We validate every schema type already in use and add what's missing.

Internal linking

We map how link equity flows through the site. Often, the pages you most want to rank are the ones with the fewest internal links pointing to them — an easy, high-impact fix once it's identified.

What happens after the audit

We don't hand you a 40-page PDF and disappear. Every finding gets ranked by estimated impact versus effort, and we start with whatever clears the biggest blockers first — usually crawlability and indexing issues, since nothing else matters if Google can't properly see your pages in the first place.

Key takeaways

  • Technical issues get fixed before content strategy — otherwise you're building on sand.
  • Crawlability and indexing problems are checked first, since they block everything else.
  • Findings are ranked by impact vs. effort, not delivered as an overwhelming checklist.
