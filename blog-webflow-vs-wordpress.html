The real difference between Webflow and WordPress for a marketing site

Not a holy war — a practical breakdown of where each one actually wins.

MW
Marcus Whitfield
Webflow & Design Lead · 8 min read

WordPress still runs a huge share of the web, and it's a perfectly capable platform. But for a new marketing site built today, we default to Webflow for most clients, and it's worth explaining exactly why rather than just asserting it.

Where Webflow wins

Design control without fighting a theme. WordPress sites are built on themes and page builders that fight you the moment you want something the theme wasn't designed for. Webflow gives visual, pixel-level control natively, which means fewer compromises between what you actually want and what the platform makes easy.

Where WordPress wins

Plugin ecosystem depth and cost. If you need a very specific, niche piece of functionality, there's a decent chance a WordPress plugin already exists for it — often free. WordPress is also typically cheaper to host at scale, and if you already have an in-house WordPress developer, there's real value in staying in a stack your team already knows.

WordPress sites are built on themes that fight you the moment you want something the theme wasn't designed for.

The maintenance burden most people don't mention

WordPress requires ongoing plugin and core updates, and skipping them is a real security risk — a huge share of hacked WordPress sites were running outdated plugins. Webflow, being a hosted platform, removes this entire category of maintenance. For a client without a dedicated developer keeping things patched, this alone is often decisive.

Editing experience for non-technical teams

This is where the gap is widest. Webflow's visual editor lets a marketing team update real content without touching code or breaking a layout. WordPress page builders can offer similar visual editing, but the experience is less consistent — quality varies enormously depending on which builder and theme combination you're running.

Our honest recommendation

New marketing site, no existing WordPress investment, non-technical team doing regular updates: Webflow, almost every time. Large existing WordPress site with custom plugins already built and working, or an in-house WordPress developer already on staff: there's a real case for staying, and we'll tell you that honestly rather than push a migration that isn't actually in your interest.

Key takeaways

  • Webflow offers more native design control and removes the plugin-update maintenance burden.
  • WordPress wins on plugin ecosystem depth and lower hosting cost at scale.
  • The right choice depends on your existing stack and whether you have in-house WordPress expertise already.
