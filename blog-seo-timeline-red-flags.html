What a real SEO timeline looks like (and why "first page in 30 days" is a red flag)

Setting honest expectations about how long organic growth actually takes.

PN
Priya Nandakumar
SEO Strategist · 8 min read

If an agency promises first-page rankings in 30 days, that's worth treating as a warning sign, not a selling point. Real, sustainable SEO takes longer than that — and understanding the real timeline helps you evaluate any agency's promises, including ours.

Months 1–2: foundation

Technical fixes, information architecture, and initial content work. You typically won't see major ranking movement yet — this phase is about removing what's actively holding the site back, which is necessary groundwork rather than a visible result in itself.

Months 3–4: early signals

Google starts re-crawling and re-evaluating fixed pages. You'll typically start seeing movement on lower-competition, longer-tail keywords first — these are genuinely useful early wins, not the full picture, but real progress worth noting.

If an agency promises first-page rankings in 30 days, that's worth treating as a warning sign, not a selling point.

Months 5–8: compounding

This is typically where the real movement happens — content published earlier starts ranking, internal linking has had time to distribute authority through the site, and competitive keywords begin shifting. This phase is also where most of the actual revenue impact starts showing up in reporting.

Months 9–12+: maturity and defense

Rankings for genuinely competitive terms solidify, and the focus shifts partly toward defending gains against competitors rather than pure growth. SEO doesn't have a finish line — it becomes an ongoing program rather than a project with a defined end date.

Why some agencies promise faster

Usually one of two reasons: they're targeting extremely low-competition, low-value keywords that were always going to be easy to rank for and won't move revenue meaningfully, or they're using tactics — paid link schemes, thin auto-generated content — that risk a Google penalty down the line. Both produce a number that looks good in month one and causes real problems later.

How to evaluate a timeline promise

Ask specifically what keywords are being targeted and why. Vague answers about "getting you to page one" without naming actual target terms and their competitiveness is itself a signal worth taking seriously before committing budget.

Key takeaways

  • Real SEO results typically build over 6–12 months, not 30 days.
  • Fast "results" often come from either low-value keywords or risky tactics that can trigger penalties later.
  • Ask any agency exactly which keywords they're targeting and why, before trusting a timeline promise.
