Webflow vs. Shopify: which one does your business actually need

Not a features comparison — a decision guide based on what you're actually trying to do.

BE
Benjamin Emmanuel
Founder, Dynatrix Tech · 9 min read

This is one of the most common questions we get before a project even starts, and most comparisons online answer it wrong — they compare features, when the real answer depends entirely on what you're selling and how.

Choose Shopify if you're selling physical products

If the core of your business is a product catalog — inventory, variants, shipping, checkout, subscriptions — Shopify wins, and it isn't close. It was built for commerce first, and it shows in everything from checkout conversion optimization to the depth of the app ecosystem for shipping, reviews, and subscriptions. Trying to bolt real ecommerce onto Webflow means rebuilding, poorly, what Shopify already does exceptionally well.

Choose Webflow if you're selling a service, not a product

Agencies, consultants, SaaS companies, real estate, hospitality — businesses where the site's job is to explain, persuade, and capture a lead or booking rather than process a cart. Webflow gives you far more design flexibility and a genuinely better content-editing experience for marketing pages, blogs, and case studies than Shopify's theme system was ever built to offer.

Trying to bolt real ecommerce onto Webflow means rebuilding, poorly, what Shopify already does exceptionally well.

The genuinely gray area: services with light ecommerce

A studio that also sells a handful of physical products (prints, merch, a small shop) doesn't always need a full Shopify migration. Webflow's ecommerce features have improved and can handle a small, simple catalog fine. The line we use: if commerce is the main event, go Shopify. If commerce is a small side feature of a mostly content-and-lead-gen site, Webflow can carry it.

What we actually ask clients

Three questions usually settle it: Will most visitors be here to browse and buy a product, or to learn about you and get in touch? Do you need subscriptions, complex variants, or heavy inventory management? And realistically, will a non-technical team member need to publish new content — blog posts, case studies — regularly? The answers almost always point clearly to one platform over the other.

The one thing both platforms have in common

Neither platform fixes a weak offer or unclear positioning. We've seen beautifully-built sites on both platforms fail to convert because the actual value proposition wasn't clear — the platform choice matters, but it's a smaller lever than most people assume relative to the quality of the strategy and content sitting on top of it.

Key takeaways

  • Shopify wins decisively for physical product ecommerce — checkout, inventory, and the app ecosystem are built for it.
  • Webflow wins for service businesses where content, design flexibility, and lead capture matter more than cart checkout.
  • The platform choice matters less than a clear offer and strong positioning sitting on top of it.
