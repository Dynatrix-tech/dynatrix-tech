Why 'mobile-first' still gets ignored on ecommerce sites

Most stores get the majority of their traffic on mobile — and design for desktop anyway.

AO
Adaeze Okon
Shopify Lead · 7 min read

For most Shopify stores we audit, mobile traffic is the clear majority — often well over half of all sessions. And yet, again and again, we find stores that were clearly designed on a desktop monitor first, with mobile treated as an afterthought that "should just work" because the theme is technically responsive.

Responsive isn't the same as mobile-first

A responsive theme resizes to fit a phone screen. A mobile-first design is built starting from the phone screen's constraints, then expanded up. The difference shows up in details: button sizes that are technically clickable but genuinely uncomfortable to tap, product image galleries that require excessive pinching and zooming, and filter menus that eat the entire screen and hide the products they're meant to help you find.

The checkout gap

This is where it matters most, and where it's most commonly overlooked. Autofill support, a properly sized keyboard for numeric fields like card numbers, and a thumb-reachable "Place order" button aren't nice-to-haves on mobile — they're the difference between a completed purchase and an abandoned cart on the platform where most of your customers actually are.

A responsive theme resizes to fit a phone screen. Mobile-first design is built starting from the phone screen's constraints.

Product photography for mobile specifically

Photos that look great as a large desktop hero image often lose all their detail crushed into a mobile-width product card. We recommend testing key product photography specifically at phone width before finalizing it, not just admiring it on a designer's large monitor during the review process.

How we actually test this

Not just resizing a browser window — we test on real devices, with real thumbs, often deliberately on an older or mid-range phone rather than the newest flagship, since that's a more honest picture of what a meaningful share of real customers are actually using.

The simplest fix most stores skip

Before any redesign, just watch someone unfamiliar with your store try to buy something on their own phone, without guiding them. The friction points reveal themselves almost immediately, and it costs nothing but twenty minutes and a bit of humility about what you assumed was obvious.

Key takeaways

  • Responsive design and true mobile-first design are not the same thing — check the details, not just whether it resizes.
  • Checkout on mobile (autofill, keyboard type, thumb-reachable buttons) is the highest-impact area to get right.
  • Test on real, mid-range devices — not just a resized browser window on a designer's monitor.
