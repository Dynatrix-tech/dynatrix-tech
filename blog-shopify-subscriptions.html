Subscriptions on Shopify: what we wish clients knew earlier

Common pitfalls when adding subscriptions to an existing store.

AO
Adaeze Okon
Shopify Lead · 9 min read

Subscriptions are one of the highest-leverage additions to an existing Shopify store — and also one of the most commonly rushed. Here's what tends to get missed.

The theme usually isn't ready

Most subscription apps bolt a widget onto your existing product page and call it done. The result often looks like an afterthought — because it is one. We treat the subscription option as a real part of the product page design, not a plugin sitting awkwardly below the "Add to cart" button.

Cancellation flow matters more than signup flow

It's tempting to focus entirely on getting people to subscribe. But a painful cancellation experience — hidden behind support emails, buried in account settings — creates resentment that shows up in chargebacks and bad reviews. A clean, honest cancellation flow actually improves retention, because people trust a subscription they know they can leave.

Inventory planning gets harder, not easier

Recurring orders sound predictable, but skips, pauses, and plan changes mean your inventory forecasting needs to account for real subscriber behavior — not just "X people times Y frequency." We build this into the reporting from day one, not as an afterthought once the numbers get confusing.

Start narrower than feels comfortable

Launching subscriptions across your entire catalog on day one is rarely the right call. We usually recommend starting with two or three genuinely repeat-purchase products, proving the mechanics work cleanly, then expanding — rather than debugging subscription logic across fifty SKUs at once.

Pricing the subscription discount correctly

A discount that's too small doesn't move anyone to subscribe. A discount that's too large quietly erodes margin on your best, most loyal customers — the ones you're least trying to discount. We usually land subscription pricing by working backward from your actual margin and customer lifetime value, not by copying whatever percentage a competitor uses.

Key takeaways

  • Design the subscription option as a real part of the product page, not a bolted-on widget.
  • An easy cancellation flow improves trust and retention — don't hide it.
  • Start with 2–3 products before rolling subscriptions out catalog-wide.
