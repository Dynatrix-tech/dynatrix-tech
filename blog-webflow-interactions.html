Five Webflow interactions that actually help conversion

And the ones that just slow your page down for no real benefit.

MW
Marcus Whitfield
Webflow & Design Lead · 6 min read

Webflow makes it dangerously easy to add motion everywhere. Some of it helps. Most of it is just decoration that costs you load time. Here's where we actually spend interaction budget.

Worth doing

  • Sticky "Add to cart" bars that appear on scroll — keeps the action available without asking the visitor to scroll back up
  • Form field validation feedback — a subtle inline check as someone fills a form reduces abandonment more than almost any visual flourish
  • Progressive image reveals on scroll — genuinely improves perceived speed when done lightly, since the page feels like it's responding to you
  • Hover states on clickable cards — a small, honest signal that something is interactive, which reduces hesitation to click
  • Loading skeletons instead of blank space while content loads — reduces the feeling of a "broken" page during load

Skip these

Full-page parallax scroll effects, elaborate text that animates in letter-by-letter, and auto-playing background video on the hero — all of these add real weight to your load time, and none of them have shown us a measurable conversion benefit worth that cost. If motion doesn't help someone understand or act faster, it's just decoration you're paying for in load speed.

The test we actually use

Before adding any interaction, we ask: does this help someone complete their task faster or with less friction? If the honest answer is "it just looks nice," it goes on the maybe-later list, not the launch list.

How to measure whether an interaction is worth keeping

Once something ships, we don't just assume it's working. Scroll-depth and click-through data on the specific element tell us within a couple of weeks whether an interaction is actually being used and helping, or just sitting there unnoticed and costing load time. Anything that isn't earning its keep gets cut in the next round, no sentimentality.

Key takeaways

  • Functional micro-interactions (validation, sticky CTAs) earn their weight; decorative ones usually don't.
  • Heavy effects like parallax and auto-play video rarely show measurable conversion benefit.
  • Measure interaction usage after launch — cut anything that isn't earning its keep.
