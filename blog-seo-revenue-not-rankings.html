Why we stopped chasing rankings and started chasing revenue

A rethink of what "SEO success" should actually mean for an ecommerce brand.

By Priya Nandakumar · 6 min read

Ranking #1 for a keyword feels good to report. It doesn't always mean much for the business. We've seen brands hit the top spot for a term that drives plenty of traffic and almost no revenue — and treat it as a win, because the number went up.

The problem with rank-first reporting

Rankings are a proxy metric, not an outcome. They're useful as a diagnostic, but they say nothing about whether the people arriving are the right people, or whether the page they land on actually converts them. A brand can "win" at SEO by this measure while genuinely losing revenue to a competitor who ranks lower but converts better.

What we track instead

Every keyword we target gets evaluated on purchase intent, not just search volume — "waterproof hiking boots size 10" beats "hiking boots" every time, even with a fraction of the traffic. We tie organic sessions to actual revenue per landing page, not just traffic totals. And we treat conversion rate on organic landing pages as seriously as the ranking position itself — a page that ranks #3 and converts at 4% often outperforms one that ranks #1 and converts at 1%.

What this changes in practice

It means we sometimes recommend targeting a "smaller" keyword over a flashier one, or spending a month fixing a landing page's conversion rate instead of chasing a new ranking. It's a less exciting story to tell, but it's the version that shows up in your actual revenue numbers — which is the only report that matters at the end of the quarter.
